Go-to-market wedge

Land where the pain is sharpest, then expand

Sell to the buyer who already holds stablecoins and feels the transparency pain most — then leverage into platforms and institutions.

Phase A · months 0–9

Crypto-native companies

Funds, trading firms, DAOs, crypto startups paying salaries, contractors, OTC in stablecoins. Fast buyers, sharp pain, tolerant of early tooling. 5–10 design partners → 20–40 paying.

Phase B · months 9–24

Fintechs & PSPs (SDK / white-label)

They embed VEIL to offer confidential stablecoin rails to their own users. One integration → many end-users. B2B2C leverage.

Phase C · year 2+

Regulated institutions

Lead with compliance — proof of innocence + auditor view keys. Longer cycles, larger contracts, cross-border B2B.

On the token

Equity first — token later, if ever

No token at seed. Build on equity + stablecoin revenue; the 2026 market punishes tokens and rewards revenue. Introduce VEIL only once there's real usage and regulatory clarity — via a foundation, as staking/fee utility, never as the fundraising mechanism.

The honest picture

Biggest risks — and why they're survivable

Risk 1

Regulatory / dev liability

  • Stay strictly non-custodial
  • Compliance-native (proof of innocence + view keys)
  • Legal opinions; engage regulators early
Risk 2

Adoption too slow

  • Blended SaaS revenue cuts volume dependence
  • Start at the sharpest-pain beachhead
  • B2B2C leverage via PSPs
Risk 3

Incumbent ships native privacy

  • Neutrality: multi-issuer, multi-chain, un-switch-off-able
  • Move first; own the brand
  • Regulator + issuer relationships

The technology risk is largely retired. What remains is execution and timing — pairing built technology with compliance-grade trust and enterprise distribution faster than incumbents bolt privacy on. That's the right kind of risk to carry.
