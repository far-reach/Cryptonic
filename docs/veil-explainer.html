Confidential stablecoin payments

VEIL / vēl /

A payment rail that lets businesses and people send digital dollars (USDC / USDT) without publishing the amount to the whole world — while still proving the money is clean and letting an auditor check the books.

In one line: a private mode for stablecoins — confidentiality, not anonymity.

The idea

What we're building, in plain terms

Stablecoins already move ~$28 trillion every quarter — but every transfer is public. A company paying salaries or suppliers on-chain exposes its payroll, margins, and counterparties to anyone with a block explorer. That single fact keeps the best payment rail ever built out of normal business use. VEIL fixes exactly that — and only that.

The problem today

Everything is visible

Send 1,000 USDC and the amount, sender, and receiver are permanent public record. No business can run finance in a glass box — so they don't use it.

What VEIL adds

A confidential mode

Amounts are hidden and the sender is unlinked from the receiver — yet a zero-knowledge proof shows the funds trace to a screened source, and a view key lets your auditor decrypt what they're allowed to see. Private to the public, open to whom you choose.

How it works

Three steps a normal person can follow

You put money in openly, it sits in a shared shielded pool where amounts are encrypted, and you take it out privately by proving — without revealing which deposit is yours — that you own clean funds.

STEP 1

Deposit

amount is visible

You move stablecoins into the pool under a secret "note." This part is public and normal — like loading a prepaid card.

Public
STEP 2

Shielded pool

amounts encrypted

Your deposit joins everyone else's inside one pool. Balances are hidden and links between deposits and withdrawals are broken.

Hidden
STEP 3

Private withdraw

proven, not revealed

You withdraw to any address by submitting a proof that you own a clean note — showing nothing about who or how much. A one-time tag stops double-spends.

Private

Compliance Proof of innocence

Withdrawals prove the money comes from an approved, screened set of deposits — so honest users show their funds aren't dirty without revealing which deposit is theirs. This is the mechanism Vitalik Buterin publicly endorsed (Privacy Pools).

Audit View keys

Each note is encrypted to a "view key." You — and anyone you hand it to, like a tax authority or auditor — can decrypt the amount. Nobody else can, and the key can't move funds. Selective disclosure you grant, not a backdoor we hold.

The end product

What you'll actually be able to use

VEIL isn't a coin to gamble on — it's infrastructure with three concrete faces.

For businesses

Confidential payments app + SDK

Pay salaries, suppliers, and contractors in stablecoins with amounts private by default. A "Stripe for confidential stablecoin payments" that drops into existing tools.

For the ecosystem

A neutral rail

An open network no single company can switch off or censor, secured by staked operators — so it can be trusted the way a public utility is.

Everywhere

Multi-chain by design

Native shielded pools on Ethereum/L2s and on Solana (Token-2022 confidential transfers), bridged so a private payment can cross chains in one action.

The honest question

Will this make money?

Short answer: the way it earns is proven and the market is enormous — but adoption is the bet, and most crypto projects don't win it. Here's the real math, not a pitch.

How it earns

Private transfer → small fee (≈0.10%) → buy back & burn VEIL

A tiny fee on each shielded transfer flows to an automatic buyback-and-burn — the same revenue→burn model the market rewards today (Hyperliquid crossed $1B in cumulative revenue doing this). No reliance on token printing.

What the numbers could look like

Stablecoins do ~$112T of volume a year. VEIL only needs a sliver of it to run private. At a 0.10% fee:

✓ Why it can

  • Stablecoins are crypto's one proven, giant market.
  • Privacy is the best-performing narrative of the cycle.
  • The earn mechanism (fee → burn) already works elsewhere.
  • Compliance-native, so businesses — the payers — can actually use it.

! Why it might not

  • Adoption may stay tiny; privacy demand is real but unproven at scale.
  • Regulatory risk around privacy tooling is unresolved.
  • Circle or a chain could ship native private stablecoins.
  • Most crypto projects, however good, still fail.
The verdict: This is a credible, revenue-generating business if it earns real adoption — not a lottery ticket, but a bet with a clear path to real cash flow and a huge ceiling. The technology risk is largely behind us (the hard part works); the remaining risk is market and regulatory. That's the right kind of risk to be taking — execution and timing, not "will the thing even function."
